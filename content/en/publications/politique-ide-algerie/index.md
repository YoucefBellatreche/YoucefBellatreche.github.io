## Abstract

For several years, Algeria has been implementing reforms to modernise the business environment and promote foreign direct investment (FDI). However, Algerian leaders have openly expressed their disappointment with the results, which fall far short of their expectations, particularly with regard to the European Union, Algeria’s leading partner. Indeed, productive European investment outside hydrocarbons accounts for only 1% of the total value of FDI in Algeria. So why are foreign investors absent from the Algerian market? To answer this question, we first review the work carried out by Algeria, which claims to have built a business-friendly environment that is attractive and less costly than in neighbouring countries; according to Algerian leaders, it is the European Union that has not kept its promises and commitments under the Association Agreement. On the other hand, the EU considers that the problem lies directly with investors, who appear not to have found a business climate offering good conditions. The EU therefore believes that further reforms are needed to improve the Algerian economic system; meanwhile, the lack of productive FDI outside hydrocarbons persists and economic development is delayed.

**Keywords:** Flows, investment, foreign direct investment, development.
